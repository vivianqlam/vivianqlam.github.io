A route through the West

Five stops. The story stays in view.

My parents’ small businesses were my first window into the decisions behind each transaction. I left Salt Lake City in 2015 for Berkeley Haas, then moved through LinkedIn in San Francisco and Asana in Portland before returning for my MBA. The places changed; I stayed curious about what customers need, where the friction is, and what the numbers can help a team do next.

  1. 01 Salt Lake City, Utah Growing up

    After school at Penny Wise Gas.

    Before opening a restaurant and a neighborhood convenience store in Salt Lake City, my parents sold baked goods by the roadside in Vietnam. They came to the U.S. as refugees and built their lives through entrepreneurship. I worked after school at Penny Wise Gas, where I watched them adapt to new technology, reconcile paper receipts, and manage thin margins. Payments were never abstract infrastructure; they shaped whether a small business had room to grow.

  2. 02 Berkeley, California 2015

    A new start at Haas.

    I left Salt Lake City in 2015 to study at UC Berkeley Haas. I spent my undergraduate summers at Morgan Stanley and Unilever, starting to see business from beyond the family store.

  3. 03 San Francisco, California 2019

    From graduation to LinkedIn.

    After graduating in 2019, I moved to San Francisco and joined LinkedIn’s Business Leadership Program as a Global Sales Associate. Working with customers and across teams showed me how much good execution depends on translating between people and priorities.

  4. 04 Portland, Oregon 2020

    A change of scenery.

    When the pandemic hit in 2020, I moved to Portland and worked remotely for Asana. I was most energized by work that started with a customer problem: analyzing account data, spotting patterns, and sharing insights with teams that could act. I also worked with Finance, Deal Desk, and Sales Operations on pricing decisions.

  5. 05 Berkeley, California Graduate school

    Back to Berkeley, looking ahead.

    I returned to Berkeley for my MBA at Haas to build a stronger analytical and strategy toolkit. During my summer 2026 internship at Visa Consulting & Analytics, I worked with financial institutions on growth and profitability questions. It felt like a natural extension of what I had seen in my parents’ businesses: use information to make the next decision clearer.
