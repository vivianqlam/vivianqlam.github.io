<section class="page-intro container">
  <p class="eyebrow">Where the questions began</p>
  <h1>Business has always felt personal to me.</h1>
  <p class="page-lede">My parents built their lives through small business. That early view of commerce is part of why I’m drawn to strategy: it connects what customers experience with what a business can do next.</p>
</section>

<div class="container content-layout">
  <article class="prose">
    <p class="drop-cap">My parents came to the U.S. as refugees after selling baked goods by the roadside in Vietnam. In Salt Lake City, they opened a restaurant and a convenience store. I worked after school at Penny Wise Gas and watched them adapt to new technology, reconcile paper receipts, and manage thin margins. Payments were never abstract infrastructure to them; they could be the difference between opportunity and instability. That stayed with me.</p>

    <p>I started my career in B2B sales, first at LinkedIn and then across five years at Asana. Working with enterprise clients showed me that the same questions about customer needs and friction come up at a much larger scale. I was most energized by the moments when I could analyze data, recognize patterns, and translate a customer’s pain point into something a cross-functional team could act on. I partnered with Revenue Operations, Finance, and Product Marketing to surface trends and refine our go-to-market approach.</p>

    <aside class="pull-quote">
      <p>Find the signal. Make it useful.</p>
    </aside>

    <p>I’m now pursuing my MBA at UC Berkeley Haas and building the analytical toolkit to work on strategy more formally. At Visa Consulting &amp; Analytics, I worked with financial institutions on growth and profitability questions. I want to keep doing work where rigorous analysis leads to practical recommendations people can use.</p>
